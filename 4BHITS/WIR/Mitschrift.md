**P 1.1 Gap-Fill Text: Stakeholders and Their Interests**

A stakeholder is any individual, group, or institution that has an **interest** (1) in a business or is affected by its activities. Businesses do not operate in **isolation** (2); their decisions influence many different groups inside and outside the company.   

Stakeholders can be divided into **internal** (3) stakeholders, such as employees and owners, and **external** (4) stakeholders, such as customers, suppliers, and the government. At the center of the stakeholder model is the **business** (5), which coordinates resources and produces goods or services.   

One important stakeholder group is the **shareholders** (6). They invest capital in the company and expect an increase in company **value** (7) as well as profits or dividends. Another key stakeholder group is **banks** (8), which provide loans and expect repayment with **interest** (9).   

**Customers** (10) buy the company’s products or services. Their main interests are reliable supply, good quality, and fair **prices** (11). If customer expectations are not met, demand may **decrease** (12).   

**Suppliers** (13) deliver raw materials or services to the company. They are interested in steady demand and timely **payment** (14). A lack of trust can harm long-term business **relationships** (15).   

**Employees** (16) are internal stakeholders who work for the company. They expect fair pay, job security, and a **safe** (17) working environment. Motivation and productivity are closely linked to working conditions.

The **government** (18) represents public interests. It expects businesses to pay **taxes** (19) and follow laws and regulations. In addition, local communities and neighbors expect no disturbances such as noise or **emissions** (20).   

Because stakeholder interests can **conflict** (21), management must balance different expectations. Effective stakeholder management helps reduce conflicts and supports **sustainable** (22) long-term business success.
